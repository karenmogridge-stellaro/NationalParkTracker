American Samoa National Park

Tropical rainforest, coral reefs, and Samoan culture on three islands in the South Pacific.

About American Samoa

The National Park of American Samoa invites you to discover the vibrant culture and stunning landscapes of the South Pacific. Experience a world unlike any other, where natural beauty and cultural heritage are protected for future generations. Join us in exploring these islands of sacred earth!

Things to do: Arts and Culture · Live Music · Hands-On · Volunteer Vacation · Hiking · Backcountry Hiking · Front-Country Hiking · Off-Trail Permitted Hiking · Living History · Historic Weapons Demonstration · First Person Interpretation · SCUBA Diving · Snorkeling · Swimming

Trails

American Samoa has roughly 4 named trails. Log the one you hiked in the app — or just the park if you're not sure.

Camping

There are no NPS campgrounds inside the park. Lodging and private campgrounds are typically available nearby.

Best time to visit

Peak season: April – November. The park is open year-round, though some roads and facilities close or run reduced hours in winter.

American Samoa is in the South Pacific Ocean, between the Equator and the Tropic of Capricorn. A tropical climate prevails with warm or hot temperatures year-round and high humidity. Rain showers are frequent and can last only for a few minutes or all-day. The average annual rainfall is 125-inches in the dryer areas to as much as 300-inches in the highest mountains.

Entrance fees

Entry is Free.

Getting there

Nearest city: Pago Pago, AS. Park center: -14.250°, -170.680°.

The visitor center is located in the village of Pago Pago, across from the Pago Way Service Station.
